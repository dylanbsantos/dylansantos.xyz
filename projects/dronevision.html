About the project

Teaching a drone to follow a face

DroneVision was my A-Level Computer Science project. I worked with a DJI/Ryze Tello drone and wrote Python programs to control its movement and use its camera feed for face tracking.

I liked the challenge of getting hardware, live video and code to work together, especially when a small change in the program produced a visible change in how the drone moved.

How I built it

Keyboard controls

I started by sending commands to the drone from the keyboard, including movement and emergency landing.

Live camera feed

I used the drone's camera feed and OpenCV to process video in real time.

Face tracking

I developed a separate script that detects a face in the camera image and adjusts drone movement to keep the face centred and at a useful distance.

The keyboard-control and face-tracking programs worked as separate scripts. I didn't finish the planned graphical interface or the toggle between control modes.

Reflection

What I learned

This project gave me hands-on experience with computer vision, testing, debugging and communicating with real hardware. It also taught me to be realistic about scope: the individual control modes worked, but the complete integrated interface was not finished.

My original A-Level report estimated command response times of approximately 0.1–0.2 seconds and around 85% face-detection accuracy during my own tests. These were informal project measurements, not independently verified benchmarks.
